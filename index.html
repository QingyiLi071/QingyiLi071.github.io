About Me

I am a second-year PhD student in the Department of Psychology at Virginia Tech. I study where individuality comes from and what it explains. Inheritance and experience unfold together across childhood and adolescence and leave traces in the brain and in daily affect. My research characterizes these traces at the level of the individual rather than the group average, focusing on brain organization and affect dynamics.

  1. Where it comes from. Using parent–child dyads, I compare parents and children on the same individualized features of brain function and structure, measured with precision functional mapping and morphometric similarity networks. This separates what the child shares with the parent, through inheritance and family environment together, from what is the child’s own.
  2. How it moves. Using hidden Markov models and intensive longitudinal data, I recover latent brain states and the dynamics of daily affect, and track how they change over time: the temporal side of the same individuality.

My ultimate goal is to use these individual-level features to predict who is at risk for internalizing problems and suicidal ideation, and to advance precision psychiatry by grounding assessment and intervention in each person’s own brain and daily experience.

Fun fact: I have been asking these questions for as long as I can remember, first through traditional Chinese divination and astrology (the I Ching, BaZi, and Zi Wei Dou Shu), systems that also try to read a person against time and circumstance. Around 24, with a freshly matured prefrontal cortex, I switched my allegiance to science. I have not forgotten my old flame: in my spare time I still study these traditions and keep refining my technique.
