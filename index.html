About Me

I am a second-year PhD student in the Department of Psychology at Virginia Tech. I am interested in how each person’s brain comes to be organized in its own way, and how that individual organization can inform mental health in adolescence.

  1. Origins of individual brain organization. Each brain has idiosyncratic features in its functional network topography that group averages blur away. I want to understand where these features come from. Using precision functional mapping, I study them in designs that speak to their origins: parent–child dyads in my current work, and twin and densely sampled individual data in the future.
  2. Brain patterns at scale. In large developmental samples, I am interested in characterizing patterns in brain dynamics and development, such as latent brain states and developmental trajectories. These patterns provide the reference against which each individual’s brain can be read.

Ultimately, I hope to translate these individual features and population patterns into precision psychiatry, grounding risk assessment and intervention in each person’s own brain.

Fun Fact

I have been curious about how a person comes to be who they are for as long as I can remember, first through traditional Chinese divination and astrology (the I Ching, BaZi, and Zi Wei Dou Shu), systems that also try to read a person against time and circumstance. Around 24, with a freshly matured prefrontal cortex, I switched my allegiance to science. I have not forgotten my old flame: in my spare time I still study these traditions and keep refining my technique.
